The hierarchy is right. What sits under it is not there yet.

Datasets and resources first, then the recommenders, then mechanisms borrowed from other fields, then ontology engineering. That order matches how I actually did the work, and I do not expect to be asked to change it.

What I do expect to be asked about is everything underneath. A capability table with no ledger behind it. A search described in two sentences. Paragraphs that summarise a paper instead of arguing with it. All three are about a week of work, and my Excel already holds most of the raw material for them.

One line diagnosis. My Excel is stronger than my chapter. The chapter is a compression of the Excel that lost the evidence on the way down.

The problem they set themselves
Content-based filtering leans on TF-IDF style similarity and collaborative filtering needs history it does not have, so neither can see how ingredients, skin types and concerns actually relate. They argue existing skincare ontologies stay shallow too — fixed one-to-one mappings, product to concern or ingredient to function, with the reasoner barely used.
Who it was built for
Indonesian skincare consumers, including people who do not know their own skin type. The system is called matchare and opens with a skin quiz.
What data they used
Scraped from three platforms: Sociolla and Beautyhaul for products, Skinsort for ingredients. Skinsort is the same source I used, which is why this paper matters to me more than its venue suggests. Supplemented by dermatological literature for clinical validity.
How much of it
Over 3,800 product entries from Sociolla and Beautyhaul, and over 28,000 ingredient records from Skinsort. Worth being precise: the 28,000 are scraped ingredient records, not 28,000 distinct substances modelled in the ontology.
Did they follow a stated method
METHONTOLOGY, named explicitly, for the ontology. Wrapped in a modified waterfall SDLC with four stages: requirement analysis, system design, implementation, testing. Requirements came from a literature review plus a user survey, then mapped against dermatologist consultation and industry labelling guidelines.
How big the ontology is
12 top-level classes  ·  25+ object properties  ·  18 data properties
Classes worth knowing
User, Product, Brand, Product Category, Allergen Type, Ingredient, Benefit, Formulation Trait, Key Ingredient, Skin Concern, Skin Type, What It Does. Product Category branches into Cleanser, Eye Care, Lip Care, Moisturizer, Set or Kit, Suncare, Treatment. Skin Type is mapped to Fitzpatrick categories.
Properties
Object: avoidFor, belongsToBrand, belongsToCategory, containsIngredient, excludeAllergenType, foundInProduct, hasFormulationTrait, hasFunction, hasProduct, hasKeyIngredient, isKeyIngredientOf, isKeyIngredientType, incompatibleWith, mayAggravateConcern, providesBenefit, providesIngredientBenefit, recommendedFor, similarTo, suitableForSkinType, synergisticWith, targetsConcern, treatsConcern, userAvoids, userHasSkinType, userPrefers.
Data: ActualFunctions, AlternativeNames, BPOMNumber, BrandName, hasAlternativeNames, hasIngredientName, HowToUse, IngredientFunction, IngredientList, IngredientName, InteractionWarnings, IsMultifunctional, LocalImagePath, MainCategory, ProductDescription, ProductName, SkinTypeNotes, Subcategory.
Rules and defined classes
None. No SWRL, no defined classes, no equivalence axioms, no reasoner profile named anywhere in the paper. What they call reasoning is multi-hop traversal of object properties in Jena. Their whole case for depth rests on chaining synergisticWith, incompatibleWith and treatsConcern, not on description logic.
Can I download it
No. No repository, no DOI for the ontology, no supplementary material. It exists in the paper as three Protégé screenshots and an OntoGraph. So my line about one of six competitors publishing anything downloadable still holds.
How the data got in
BeautifulSoup scripts, no mapping language. No RML, no R2RML, no OntoRefine — scraped into the knowledge base and integrated through the OWL API. Preprocessing: duplicates removed, ingredient names standardised to a controlled vocabulary, missing values imputed with domain heuristics, and extreme ingredient counts flagged for manual review. They say adaptive parsing and respectful delays handled inconsistent HTML and rate limiting.
Tools, and what each was for
Protégé to build. Apache Jena Fuseki as the reasoning engine, SPARQL as the query language, OWL API to integrate the backend. React frontend, Node.js backend, PostgreSQL alongside the graph for user feedback and interaction logs.
How it actually recommends
The user either takes the skin quiz or types in concerns and sensitivities. That input is converted to RDF triples against the ontology — their worked example is skin type dry, concern acne, sensitivity alcohol. Logical inference then runs over suitableForSkinType, treatsConcern, incompatibleWith and avoidFor to return a filtered list. The same shape as mine: constraint, not ranking.
How they tested it
Functional testing, then the Technology Acceptance Model. Ten respondents, five-point Likert, two constructs only: Perceived Usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use. Mixed ages and skin types, majority female, some unsure of their own skin type.
What they got, and does it hold up
Average 4.5 out of 5, SD 0.3. Perceived Usefulness 4.8 / 4.4 / 4.6, Perceived Ease of Use 4.4 / 4.3 / 4.4. Highest single item, 4.8, is "the system helps me find suitable products". No accuracy, no precision, no recall, no baseline, no ground truth, no held-out set. It holds up as an acceptance study of ten people and nothing more — but their discussion then claims superiority over content-based and collaborative filtering, which nothing they measured supports.

My ontology — verdict and final structure

I read the draft in my "My ontology" sheet. Short answer: it is sound, and better than four of the six competitor ontologies in my review. Three changes make it defensible rather than merely sensible.

What is right
  • Four modules by rate of change — this is MOMo, and Hansanie justified the same split
  • Offer separate from Product, so one lotion carries two Beirut prices
  • IngredientListing as a reified link, which is what lets position exist
  • Claim carrying agent, sentence, date and tier
  • Predicting membership counts before running the reasoner
What to change
  • Rename SensitiveSafeProduct — it asserts safety the axiom cannot support
  • Add an explicit completeness flag per product, or the negative classes are unsound
  • Split Claim into what the source said and what you derived — Jannatin's two class names
  • Promote ConsumerNeed out of the Lebanon module; it is domain-level, not market-level
What is missing
  • No versioning on the allergen list — it changed in 2023 and will change again
  • No BodySite class, though four product types imply one and DermO would supply it
  • No stated OWL profile. Mine is OWL 2 DL; say so in the chapter
  • No maintenance plan, which LOT requires as a stage
The one that matters most. SensitiveSafeProduct ≡ Product ⊓ ¬∃contains.DeclarableAllergen does not do what it looks like it does. Under OWL's open-world assumption, a product whose formula you never recorded is indistinguishable from one that contains nothing declarable — so 827 products with no formula at all would classify as "safe". Rename it NoListedAllergenDetected, qualify it with the list version, and scope it to products carrying a complete formula. A jury member who knows OWL will go straight here.

Semantic web checklist

Every concept my pipeline needs, what it does, and where you stand. If I can explain each row in two sentences I can hold any conversation about the semantic web side of this thesis.

RDF — the triple
Subject, predicate, object. Every fact in my graph is one of these. It matters because a subject identified by an IRI can be referred to by anyone else's data — that is the whole mechanism by which my ingredient connects to the European register.
IRIs and why you mint my own
A name that is also an address. My products get w3id.org IRIs so they survive my graduation; my ingredients get CosIng's IRIs, because reusing someone's identifier is what linking means.
RDFS — subclass, domain, range
The light vocabulary. Gets you the concern hierarchy, so a query for "irritation" returns "redness" without me listing every child term. This is Middleton's inference-up-the-hierarchy finding, cheaply.
OWL 2 DL and the defined class
Membership stated as an equivalence axiom instead of tagged product by product. My six classes. Name the profile in my chapter — mine is DL, and no paper in my review states theirs.
Open-world assumption and closure
The absence of a triple is not a statement of absence. This is why my negative classes need a completeness flag and an explicit closure mechanism — a cardinality restriction, an enumeration, or a screening step outside the reasoner. Decide which before I write Chapter 5.
Reasoning: classification, consistency, instance checking
Three different jobs. Classification fills my defined classes; consistency turns a contradiction in my data into an error instead of a plausible wrong answer; instance checking is what actually produces a recommendation.
SHACL — validation, and what OWL cannot do
OWL never reports an error because it has no closed world; it just infers something I did not expect. SHACL checks a graph conforms and names the node that broke the rule. It can also count, which OWL cannot. Zero of 28 reviewed studies used it — this is free differentiation.
SPARQL, including CONSTRUCT and named graphs
SELECT answers my competency questions. CONSTRUCT writes new triples, which is how Abesová built her score triples. GRAPH restricts a query to one source, which is how a provenance-aware query works.
Named graphs vs reification vs RDF-star
Three ways to say something about a triple. You have chosen named graphs per source, which is right — but I need the Sikos survey in §9 to show you compared the alternatives rather than picked one.
PROV-O
The W3C standard for where a fact came from: wasDerivedFrom, wasAttributedTo, generatedAtTime. Expressing my four evidence levels in these terms is what lets somebody else's provenance tooling read them.
SKOS
For controlled lists that are words rather than logic. My benefits are terms, not classes. It also gives me altLabel, which a Lebanese dataset genuinely needs for Arabic.
R2RML / RML mapping
A declarative file saying which column becomes which property. The point is not convenience — it is that a mapping is a publishable artefact and a script is not.
Competency questions
The ontology is specified by the questions it must answer. Each becomes a SPARQL query, and that pairing is my functional evaluation. Write them before modelling, not after.
Vocabulary reuse and MIREOT
Import the minimum set of terms with their provenance rather than swallowing a whole ontology. I need this for DermO — importing 3,000 disease terms to use six would wreck my reasoning time and my comprehensibility.
FAIR publication
Findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable. A permanent identifier, a licence, generated documentation, a DOI per release. FOOPS! scores it for you. One of six competitors manages this.

What the jury will ask

Twelve questions, ordered by how likely they are. The answers are short on purpose — a confident two sentences beats a nervous paragraph.

Why an ontology? Why not just train a model?
Because the output has to carry a reason a pharmacist can check. Lee et al. do this task with a Transformer and get a weight; I get the ingredient, its recognised function and the annex entry it falls under. Tarus, with 277 citations, shows ontological domain knowledge also relieves cold start — and in Lebanon every user is a cold-start user. I would concede that with enough labelled data a model likely wins on raw accuracy; that is not the goal I set.
Why not just ask an LLM?
Ali et al. 2026 measured exactly this in clinical question answering: GPT-4 hallucinated on about 63% of answers, and the same questions over an ontology-grounded graph came back 98% correct with 1.7% hallucination. Skincare is safety-relevant in the same way. The ontology is not an alternative to a language model, it is the grounding that makes one usable — and that is my future work chapter.
You have no users and no interaction data. How is this a recommender system?
It is a knowledge-based recommender, which is the one family whose input condition I can satisfy. Guo's TKDE survey classifies methods for using a knowledge graph and assumes one already exists for the domain — for skincare in a local market it does not. This thesis builds it. That places the work upstream of the survey rather than failing its assumptions.
What exactly is new here? Abesová already did skincare with defined classes.
She did, and I credit it — her defined-class method is the single technique I took most directly. Her third stated limitation is, in her words, that class restrictions based on ingredients could be developed for a more symbolic and chemical approach. That is my contribution written as future work in her paper. Her classes rest on review scores; mine rest on the formula and on the EU register.
Why did you choose MOMo and LOT over METHONTOLOGY?
Two of the seven skincare ontologies I reviewed named METHONTOLOGY and four named nothing at all. I needed a method that treats publication as a stage, because one of six competitors publishes anything downloadable — that is LOT. And I needed modules, because my four parts change at completely different speeds: products weekly, regulation yearly, clinical knowledge almost never. That is MOMo. Noy and McGuinness do address reuse in their step 2, so I do not reject them for that; they simply do not prescribe the artefacts I have to produce.
Why SHACL as well as OWL? Isn't that redundant?
They do opposite jobs. OWL derives what follows under an open world and never reports an error — if my data contradicts itself, OWL infers something surprising rather than complaining. SHACL checks conformance under a closed reading and names the row that broke the rule. It can also count, which OWL cannot. None of the 28 studies in Rahayu's review used it, so it is also the cheapest thing that distinguishes this work.
My SensitiveSafeProduct class — does that actually work under OWL semantics?
As originally written, no, and I changed it. Absence of an allergen triple is not a statement that no allergen is present, so 827 products with no formula would have classified as safe. It is now NoListedAllergenDetected, scoped to products flagged as carrying a complete formula, qualified by the version of the allergen list, and closed explicitly rather than by assumption. The rename matters too — a complete declaration without the listed substances does not establish safety for any individual person.
How do I know my ingredient matching is correct?
I measured it rather than assumed it. Matching to the register is exact after lowercasing and bracket removal — nothing fuzzy, because a near miss on a chemical name is a different chemical. For the product matching I read every recovery from the third-party route by hand: five of 27 were wrong and four more doubtful, an error rate of 19 to 33%. That measurement is why the evidence tiers exist. I should be clear that 96.6% is coverage, not accuracy — the correctness of a sample of resolutions is still to be measured.
Why should a thesis be judged on building a dataset?
FoodKG presented construction itself as the contribution and ISWC accepted it. CCIBP did the same in Bioinformatics. And the alternative was not available: no resource exists that carries marketed products and regulatory grounding together, so the ontology had nothing to be built over until this one existed.
Isn't focusing on Lebanon too narrow for a thesis?
Lahoud et al. published a Lebanon-focused ontology recommender in Education and Information Technologies and it has 36 citations, so local scope is publishable. More to the point, the constraint is the contribution: availability is a binding constraint here in a way it is not in the markets every other system assumes, and that forces availability into the model rather than leaving it outside.
Which OWL profile are you using, and why?
OWL 2 DL. I need negation and property restrictions for the defined classes, which rules out EL and QL — ELK would be considerably faster and cannot express them. HermiT handles DL and ships with Protégé. Worth noting that not one paper in my review states its profile at all.
My dataset says prices carry a date. Do they?
For the international catalogue, yes — 5,453 of them. For the Lebanese records, essentially no: ten retail records and six origin records, and none of the 519 products carried by both sources. That is the weakest point in the release and I state it as a limitation rather than a property. The listing itself is evidence of availability at collection time, and the collection window is documented, but a per-product date for local prices is future work.
